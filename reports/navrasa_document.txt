Abstract. Prevailing emotion recognition systems operate within the
categorical boundaries inherited from Western psychological models, of-
ten unable to account for experiential states central to Indian aesthetic
traditions. This paper introduces NavrasaNet, a deep learning system
designed for real time recognition of emotional states organised accord-
ing to the Navarasa theory of Bharata Muni's Natya Shastra. Built upon
the Navarasa Facial Expression Corpus (NFEC), a purpose built
dataset developed in collaboration with classically trained Indian dance
practitioners, thus ensuring both cultural and technical credibility. A
critical portion of the dataset's foundational ground truth was provided
by ICCR empanelled artist Smt. Anita Babu, bringing immense expres-
sive authenticity to the corpus.Furthermore, to strengthen credibility of
the dataset, it was veried by the government of India recognised scholar
and researcher, Dr. Manorama B.N.. To capture subtle facial geome-
tries while avoiding overtting, we employ an EcientNet B2 architec-
ture utilizing a two phase ne tuning strategy with dierential learning
rates, augmented by label smoothing and dropout. Evaluating the model
under a Leave One Subject Out (LOSO) cross validation scheme to en-
sure generalization to unseen performers, NavrasaNet achieves a peak
validation accuracy of 93.43% and a robust test accuracy of 87.32% (with
a macro averaged F1 score of 87%). Furthermore, the framework demon-
strates practical utility through a Vedic Raga Recommender application.

Introduction
Emotion recognition is a well established domain within aective computing and
articial intelligence. However, the categorical structures that underpin modern
systems remain largely unchanged since the advent of universalist models and
dimensional circumplex theories. Originating predominantly from a single cul-
tural and epistemological tradition, these frameworks create a systemic blind

spot. Aective states culturally signicant within the Indian context fall almost
entirely outside the representational capacity of current computational systems.
The Navarasa framework, formalised in the Natya Shastra and philosophi-
cally deepened by Abhinavagupta in the Abhinavabharati, identies nine founda-
tional states: Shringara (love), Hasya (mirth), Karuna (sorrow), Raudra (fury),
Veer (heroism), Bhayanaka (terror), Bibhatsa (disgust), Adbhuta (wonder), and
Shanta (serenity). Each state is associated with specic physiognomic markers
or Sthayi Bhavas, preserved through continuous pedagogical transmission across
generations of classical performing artists. This unbroken lineage has produced
practitioners whose expressive output carries the rigour of a structured, super-
vised training curriculum.
NavrasaNet seeks to bring this profound knowledge into the computational
domain. We present a culturally grounded pipeline combining rigorous dataset
curation with modern deep learning architectures. Specically, we utilize an E-
cientNet B2 backbone to eectively capture subtle facial micro expressions with-
out the parameter bloat of traditional deep convolutional networks. Furthermore,
we demonstrate the real world applicability of this emotion recognition system
through the development of a Vedic Raga Recommender Raga Ratnakar
: Where Every Mood Finds a Raga website, which utilizes the user's inferred
Navarasa state to suggest contextually resonant musical compositions.

The Navarasa Facial Expression Corpus
Existing benchmarks for facial aect recognition such as FER2013 or AectNet
are constructed exclusively around Western emotion categories and contain no
culturally annotated samples representing Indian aesthetic traditions. To ad-
dress this gap, we developed the Navarasa Facial Expression Corpus (NFEC) in
partnership with performing arts institutions and individual practitioners whose
credentials are recognized at the national level by the Government of India.
2.1
Curation and Authenticity
A foundational portion of the corpus was contributed by Smt. Anita Babu,
an ICCR empanelled artist trained under Padmashree Guru Gangadhar Prad-
han and Founder of Odissi Natya Sala, New Delhi. Her command of classical
expressions brings immense credibility to the project ensuring that the emo-
tions captured reect the precision demanded by decades of rigorous training.
Furthermore to ensure authenticity, the dataset's expressive ground truth was
veried by Dr. Manorama B.N., Director, Researcher, and Author at Noopura
Bhramari  an accredited Centre of Indian Knowledge System (IKS) under the
IKS Division, Govt of India. Her extensive background in performing, teaching,
researching and publishing multimedia literature in the realm of Indian Perform-
ing Arts establishes a rigorous academic and cultural validation layer. Additional
contributions from practitioners across multiple classical dance forms (such as
Ms. Achuta Manasa) provide cross tradition coverage and meaningful varia-
tion within each category.

2.2
Dataset Statistics and Split Strategy
The NFEC consists of exactly 21,752 carefully extracted and annotated frames.
Traditional frame extraction via OpenCV frequently resulted in temporal cor-
ruption and early EOF errors. Thus, a custom byte level extraction pipeline
utilizing FFmpeg was engineered to guarantee the delity of the facial geome-
tries.
To rigorously test the generalizability of the model against demographic bi-
ases, the dataset also utilised Leave One Subject Out (LOSO) cross vali-
dation paradigm. The data was split into Training (14,805 images), Validation
(3,635 images), and Testing (3,312 images). The test split contains unseen per-
formers, ensuring that the network learns the universal geometric structures of
the Navrasas rather than merely memorizing the identities of the training sub-
jects.
3
Computational Framework
Initial experiments on our custom dataset highlighted the immense diculty
of culturally specic emotion recognition. When building a standard convolu-
tional network entirely from scratch, performance rapidly collapsed to random
guessing (approximately 14% test accuracy). Conversely, transferring heavy ar-
chitectures like ResNet 50 resulted in severe catastrophic overtting: while the
model achieved 98% accuracy on the validation set, it plummeted to a mere 60%
on the test set, demonstrating a complete failure to generalize across dierent
performers. To overcome these limitations and optimize the parameter to accu-
racy ratio for subtle facial feature extraction, NavrasaNet specically employs
an EcientNet B2 architecture.
3.1
Model Architecture
EcientNet relies on compound scaling, uniformly scaling depth, width and
resolution to capture ne grained facial geometries (such as the arching of an
eyebrow in Adbhuta or the aring of nostrils in Raudra) far more eectively than
older architectures. The pre trained ImageNet classier head was discarded and
replaced with a densely connected custom head comprising dropout layers and
a nal 9 class Softmax layer corresponding to the Navarasa states.
3.2
Two Phase Training Strategy
Training deep networks on highly subjective emotional data frequently leads to
catastrophic forgetting or early plateauing. To mitigate this, a two phase ne
tuning strategy was implemented:
1. Phase 1 (Warmup): The EcientNet B2 backbone is frozen and only the
randomly initialized custom classication head is trained. This isolates the
backbone's pre learned texture detection capabilities from being destroyed
by initially large, unstructured error gradients.

2. Phase 2 (Full Fine Tuning): The entire network is unfrozen. To en-
sure stable convergence, we employ a Cosine Annealing Learning Rate
Scheduler paired with dierential learning rates (backbone at 1 × 10−5
and classication head at 1 × 10−4). The cosine decay smoothly anneals the
learning rate, allowing the network to traverse the complex loss landscape
eciently without abrupt magnitude drops, ultimately settling into an op-
timal local minimum. Additionally, gradient clipping is applied to stabilize
weight updates during the initial transition into full ne tuning.
3.3
Regularization for Subjective Emotion
Emotion is phenomenologically ambiguous; a practitioner portraying Raudra
(Anger) may exhibit microscopic, overlapping traces of Veer (Heroism). To ac-
count for this inherent subjectivity, we employ Label Smoothing (α = 0.1).
This prevents the network from over calibrating to single class hard assign-
ments, training the model to respect the continuous spectrum of aective states.
Furthermore, aggressive spatial augmentations (Random Resized Crops, Color
Jittering and Rotations) coupled with targeted dropout regularization (p = 0.4
and p = 0.3 in the dual layer classication head) enforce strict background and
demographic invariance.

Experiments and Results
The model was trained on cloud based GPU instances with custom fault toler-
ance mechanisms to survive network interruptions. An automatic early stopping
protocol halted training when validation accuracy plateaued, nalizing optimal
model weights at Epoch 29.

Application: The Vedic Raga Recommender
The ability to computationally detect these culturally specic states opens con-
crete avenues for human computer interaction. We have developed a full stack

web application, Raag Ratnakar, which directly leverages the NavrasaNet
pipeline in real time. As demonstrated in Figure 9 and 10, by capturing the
user's current facial expression via a webcam interface, the system infers their
prevailing Navarasa state and subsequently recommends a contextually appropri-
ate musical composition (Raga) from the Indian classical tradition. This system
eectively grounds the digital listening experience in ancient classical correspon-
dences, oering a personalized, culturally responsive environment that dynami-
cally adapts to the user's aective state.
5.1
System Architecture
Raag Ratnakar is engineered as a modular, service oriented full stack web appli-
cation. The backend exposes a RESTful API layer through which the following
services are orchestrated:
1. Rasa Predictor Service: The NavrasaNet model is utilised to detect user's
current mood. Incoming webcam frame is pre processed (Resize to 256, Cen-
terCrop to 224) and fed through the network to yield a Navarasa classica-
tion.
2. Environment Service: Computes the user's temporal and seasonal context
(detailed in Section 5.2).
3. Mode Engine: Implements the recommendation strategy (Traditional or
Therapeutic, detailed in Section 5.3).
4. Rasa Repository: Maintains an indexed lookup of Raga to Sthayi Bhava
mappings, enabling ecient retrieval of candidate Ragas by emotional an-
ity, seasonal association and temporal suitability.
5. Raga Recommender: Aggregates the outputs of the above services and
applies a weighted scoring function (Section 5.4) to rank and return the nal
Raga recommendations.
6. Authentication Service: Manages user registration and session handling
via JWT tokens with bcrypt hashed passwords, ensuring secure and persis-
tent user proles.
5.2
Uddipana: Environmental Emotional Triggers
A central innovation of Raag Ratnakar is its incorporation of Uddipana (exci-
tants), i.e. environmental stimuli that amplify or modulate the listener's emo-
tional state. The Natya Shastra recognizes that aesthetic experience is not solely
a function of an internal emotional disposition but is deeply conditioned by the
surrounding environment. Two classical dimensions of Uddipana are computa-
tionally operationalized:
Prahara (Time of Day): Indian classical music prescribes specic perfor-
mance times for each Raga, rooted in the concept of Samay (time). The system
determines the user's current Prahara by rst obtaining the device's GPS coor-
dinates (latitude, longitude) and computing the exact local sunrise and sunset

times. The resulting daylight and nighttime durations are each divided into four
equal segments, yielding the eight classical Praharas dynamically calibrated to
the user's geographic location rather than relying on xed clock hours. This en-
sures that the temporal context remains astronomically accurate regardless of
the user's position on the globe.
Ritu (Season): To capture the seasonal dimension, the system queries the
Open Meteo weather API using the user's coordinates to retrieve live mete-
orological data encoded as WMO weather codes. These codes are mapped to
qualitative weather conditions (e.g. WMO code 80 corresponds to a monsoon
like rainfall pattern). Simultaneously, the current calendar month is converted
to the corresponding Indian Ritu (e.g., Varsha, Sharad, Hemanta). A weather
match score is then computed by cross referencing the API derived weather
mood against the calendrical Ritu, capturing the degree of alignment between
the actual atmospheric conditions and the classical seasonal ontology.
5.3
Recommendation Modes
Raag Ratnakar oers two philosophically distinct recommendation strategies,
each grounded in classical Indian aesthetic theory:
Traditional Mode (ISO Principle + Sadharanikarana): This mode op-
erates on the ISO Principle of music therapy, which advocates matching the
listener's current emotional state before gradually transitioning them. If the
user is experiencing Krodha (anger), the system rst recommends a Raga whose
dominant Bhava resonates with anger, meeting the listener where they are emo-
tionally. The philosophical justication derives from Abhinavagupta's concept
of Sadharanikarana (universalization of emotion): fully experiencing an emo-
tion within an aesthetic frame is itself cathartic and healing, enabling a natural
progression toward equanimity.
Therapeutic Mode: In contrast, the therapeutic strategy pivots immediately
to a positive emotion at an equivalent energy level then gradually eases the
listener toward a state of calm. This mode assigns equal weight (50/50) between
energy matching and valence correction, prioritizing immediate mood elevation.
A disclaimer accompanies this mode, clarifying that it is not a substitute for
professional mental health support and is intended solely as an aesthetic wellness
tool.
5.4
Scoring and Ranking
Once the Mode Engine determines the target emotion, candidate Ragas from
the repository are ranked using a composite weighted score:

• Bhava Match (70%): Quanties the alignment between the Raga's pri-
mary or secondary Sthayi Bhava and the target emotion generated by the
Mode Engine.
• Ritu & Weather Match (20%): Evaluates the correspondence between
prevailing meteorological conditions and the Raga's traditional seasonal as-

• Prahara Match (10%): Veries that the user's local time of day falls

This weighting ensures that emotional resonance remains the dominant selec-
tion criterion while environmental and temporal context provide meaningful

Conclusion
NavrasaNet presents a computationally grounded approach to culturally specic
emotion recognition, supported by a practitioner validated corpus whose anno-
tation quality derives from expert consensus within living classical traditions.
By engineering a custom data pipeline, leveraging the compound scaling of E-
cientNet B2 and utilising LOSO cross validation, we achieved 87.32% recognition
accuracy across nine subjective states. Future work aims to expand the frame-
work into a multimodal system incorporating vocal prosody and gestural cues,
reecting the holistic nature of Indian aesthetics.
