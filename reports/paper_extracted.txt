Abstract RagaMoodSync presents an innovative frame-
work for personalized music therapy through Hindustani
Raga recommendations based on facial emotion recogni-
tion. Utilizing a Fusion-DenseNet (FusDenseNet) archi-
tecture with advanced multi-pooling for emotion detection,
the system achieves high precision in identifying user
moods. Integrated with a Focus Deep Collaborative Fil-
tering (FDCF) recommendation system, RagaMoodSync
delivers tailored raga suggestions that address emotional
well-being and stress management. Validated with the
FER2013 dataset, the framework demonstrates superior
performance over existing approaches. By merging AI-
driven emotion recognition with the therapeutic potential
of Indian classical music, this system pioneers a novel
intersection of technology and mental health care, offering
a promising pathway for non-invasive emotional healing
solutions.

1 Introduction
Human interaction is an essential part of daily life, where
communication through words, tones, and emotions facil-
itates the exchange of vast information. Among nonverbal
communication methods, facial expressions play a signiﬁ-
cant role as they convey emotions such as happiness,
sadness, surprise, and neutrality, driven by variations in
facial muscles [ 1, 2]. Emotion recognition has become a
cornerstone in areas like face detection, pattern recogni-
tion, and computer vision, with applications in surveil-
lance, security, adaptive interfaces, and multimedia
environments [3, 4]. Recognizing emotions involves a two-
step process: identifying facial features and analyzing the
emotions conveyed [ 5].
Parallel to facial expression analysis, music has emerged
as a universal medium for emotional communication.
Attributes like tempo, pitch, and volume are key indicators
of a song’s emotional tone. For instance, faster tempo and
major keys often signify happiness, while slower tempo
and minor keys convey sadness [ 6]. Music can inﬂuence
mood and arousal, with research emphasizing its thera-
peutic effects in reducing stress and managing cognitive
disorders through applications like music therapy [ 7].
North Indian Classical Music (NICM) stands out for its
ability to evoke emotions, as ragas—a unique aspect of
NICM—blend traditional Persian music and Vedic chant-
ing. Ragas create speciﬁc emotional atmospheres, with
components like alaap (slow, rhythmic-free introductions)
and gat (faster sections with rhythmic cycles) enhancing
emotional engagement [ 8, 9]. Recent studies have also

2 Related work
Emotion recognition and music recommendation systems
have seen signiﬁcant advancements with the integration of
deep learning and AI technologies. Sarkar et al. [ 16] uti-
lized Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for emotion
classiﬁcation in Indian classical music, achieving enhanced
accuracy and feature extraction. However, the study faced
limitations due to dataset inadequacies. Mathur et al. [ 1]
highlighted the emotional responses evoked by Hindustani
ragas, emphasizing the role of musical structure in inﬂu-
encing emotions. Similarly, Valla et al. [ 2] examined the
relationship between North Indian Classical Music (NICM)
and emotional states, showing how cultural context impacts
musical emotion experiences.
Midya et al. [ 3] explored cultural differences in per-
ceiving musical emotions, while Gulati et al. [ 4] investi-
gated the disparity between perceived and induced
emotions in Hindustani music. Research by Ravi and
Govindaraj [5] further demonstrated the emotional impact
of speciﬁc Carnatic ragas. On the technological front,
Khalil et al. [ 8] reviewed deep learning approaches for
speech emotion recognition, highlighting their applicability
to music emotion detection. Micallef and Eerola [ 7]
introduced an interactive system for real-time control of
musical emotions, bridging the gap between AI and user-
centered applications.
Studies like Sengupta et al. [ 9] delved into the neural
responses to Hindustani ragas using EEG, illustrating their
potential in emotion quantiﬁcation. Nagarajan et al. [ 10]
analyzed the immediate effects of Indian music on atten-
tion and concentration, while Beier et al. [ 11] conducted
cross-cultural studies to understand emotional responses to
music. Advanced neural network architectures, such as
those by Krishna et al. [ 21] and Arkachaari et al. [ 17],
combined facial emotion recognition with music recom-
mendation for personalized experiences. These studies
underscore the potential of integrating music emotion
analysis with therapeutic applications, making NICM a
unique avenue for stress and emotion management.
The use of deep learning techniques like transfer
learning (Punuri et al. [ 24]) and attention-based models
(Roy et al. [ 26]) has further enhanced the precision of
facial emotion recognition systems. Recent works also
address the challenges posed by distorted images (Her-
nandez-Luquin et al. [ 27]) and low-resolution datasets
(Bodavarapu and Srinivas [ 29]), offering robust solutions
for real-world applications. Overall, the amalgamation of
deep learning, cultural music insights, and therapeutic
applications represents a promising interdisciplinary ﬁeld,
paving the way for innovative tools like the Raga-
MoodSync framework.

3.1 Facial emotion recognition
The ﬁrst step in the proposed system is to recognize the
user’s emotional state through facial expressions. This is
achieved by using an advanced deep learning model,
Fusion-DenseNet (FusDenseNet) , which is designed to
extract and analyze facial features with high precision.
FusDenseNet is based on the concept of cross-dense
connections, enhancing feature extraction through its
multi-pooling architecture, which improves the recognition
of subtle emotional cues in facial expressions.

4 FusDenseNet model integration
The FusDenseNet architecture leverages the advantages of
deep neural networks (DNNs) and attention-based mecha-
nisms for enhanced feature extraction. Key beneﬁts of
FusDenseNet include:
• Improved feature extraction capability.
• Robustness in handling complex data relationships.
• Effective modeling of spatial relations in music feature
attributes.
Each layer in FusDenseNet is concatenated with the
previous layer’s output, ensuring:
Feature Reuse : Intermediate features are preserved and
reused.
Gradient Mitigation : Addresses vanishing gradient
issues.
Feature Propagation : Strengthens information ﬂow
across layers.
Dense connections enable the middle layer to retain
essential features, contributing to robust and comprehen-
sive representations of user preferences and music data.
4.1 Dataset description
The proposed system was validated using two datasets: the
Facial Emotion Recognition 2013 (FER2013) dataset and a
custom-created Hindustani Raga dataset. The FER2013
dataset, widely used for emotion recognition tasks, com-
prises over 34,000 labeled grayscale images representing
various facial expressions such as happiness, sadness, and
neutrality. This dataset has been instrumental in bench-
marking state-of-the-art emotion recognition models and
was introduced in [ 30]. Additionally, a Hindustani Raga
dataset was speciﬁcally curated for this work, featuring 12
ragas performed by professional musicians using instru-
ments like the ﬂute, guitar, and harmonium. Emotional
ratings were collected for each raga to map them to speciﬁc
moods, such as happiness and sadness, following the
methodology outlined in [ 1]. These datasets enabled a
comprehensive evaluation of the proposed system’s per-
formance in recognizing emotions and recommending
therapeutic music.
4.2 Performance metrics
The performance of the proposed system is evaluated using
metrics such as precision, recall, F-measure, speciﬁcity,
True Positive Rate (TPR), False Positive Rate (FPR), False
Rejection Rate (FRR), and Area Under the Curve (AUC).
Accuracy measures the proportion of correct predictions
relative to the total predictions and is calculated as the ratio
of true positives and true negatives to the total number of
instances. Precision focuses on the exactness of the clas-
siﬁcation and is deﬁned as the ratio of true positives to the
sum of true positives and false positives. Recall, also
known as sensitivity, indicates the model’s ability to detect
relevant data and is the ratio of true positives to the sum of
true positives and false negatives. Speciﬁcity measures the
system’s capability to correctly identify non-anomalous
instances by calculating the ratio of true negatives to the
sum of true negatives and false positives.
The True Positive Rate (TPR) quantiﬁes the ability to
correctly predict positive cases, while the False Positive
Rate (FPR) measures the probability of misclassifying a
negative instance as positive. The loss function evaluates
the number of incorrect predictions and provides insights
into model efﬁciency. Finally, the Receiver Operating
Characteristic (ROC) curve visualizes the trade-off
between TPR and FPR across different thresholds, with the
Area Under the Curve (AUC) serving as a metric to assess
the overall performance of the classiﬁcation model. A
higher AUC indicates better system performance.

5 Conclusion
In this paper, an enhanced FusDenseNet model with
Multi-Pooling layers was developed to recognize emotions
such as happiness, sadness, tensed/restless, and neutral
from facial images. Following this, a novel Focus Deep
Collaborative Filtering (FDCF) recommendation system
was proposed, which recommends Hindustani ragas tai-
lored to users’ emotional states. The advanced attention
mechanism and feature preservation methods ensured
superior performance compared to other deep learning
methods.
